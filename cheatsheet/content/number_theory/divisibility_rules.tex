\subsection{Divisibility Rules}

\subsubsection{7}

To check if a number is divisible by 7, follow this rule:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Take the number in question.
  \item Remove the last digit (units place) from the number.
  \item Double the value removed in the previous step.
  \item Subtract the doubled value from the remaining number.
  \item If the result of the subtraction is divisible by 7, the original number is divisible by 7.
\end{enumerate}

Example:

Suppose we want to check if the number 413 is divisible by 7.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Remove the last digit (3) and double it, getting 6.
  \item Subtract 6 from the remaining number (41 - 6 = 35).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{11}

\[
n \text{ is divisible by 11} \iff \sum_{i=1}^{k} a_{2i-1} - \sum_{i=1}^{j} a_{2i} \text{ is divisible by 11}
\]

where \(a_{i}\) is the \(i\)-th digit of number \(n\), \(k\) is the count of odd-positioned digits, \(j\) is the count of even-positioned digits.

Example:

Suppose we want to check if the number \(n = 7923\) is divisible by 11.

\[
k = 2, \quad j = 2
\]

\[
\text{Sum of odd-positioned digits: } 7 + 3 = 10
\]

\[
\text{Sum of even-positioned digits: } 9 + 2 = 11
\]

\[
\text{Subtraction: } 10 - 11 = -1
\]

Since \(-1\) is not divisible by 11, the number \(7923\) is not divisible by 11.

\subsubsection{13}

\begin{equation}
    13 | x \equiv 13 | 4 \cdot (x\%10) + \lfloor x/10 \rfloor
\end{equation}

In other words, 13 divides $x$ if four times the last digit plus the number without this digit is divisible by 13.

\subsubsection{17}

\begin{equation}
    17 | x \equiv 17 |  \lfloor x/10 \rfloor - 5 \cdot (x \% 10)
\end{equation}

In other words, 17 divides $x$ if the difference between the number without the last digit and five times the last digit is divisible by 17.

\subsubsection{19}

\begin{equation}
    19 | x \equiv 19 | \lfloor x / 10 \rfloor + 2 \cdot (x \mod 10)
\end{equation}

In other words, 19 divides $x$ if twice the last digit of $x$ plus the remaining number is divisible by 19.

\subsubsection{23}

\begin{equation}
    23 | x \equiv 23 | \lfloor x / 10 \rfloor + 7 \cdot (x \mod 10)
\end{equation}

In other words, 23 divides $x$ if the number without the last digit plus seven times the last digit is divisible by 23.
